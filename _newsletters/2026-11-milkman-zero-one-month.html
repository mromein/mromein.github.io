Hi!

Wowee. One month from today, MILKMAN ZERO opens. I've spent the fall in Omaha, Nebraska, doing an artist residency at the Bemis Center for Contemporary Arts, writing, coding, testing, and generally getting the milk ready for delivery. 👍🏻

Last week we ran the whole thing for the Bemis staff and residents. I don't want to give anything away, but the words people used afterward were “trance-like,” “hypnotic,” and “a little fucked up.” I'll take it.

I'm back in NYC on November 13, rehearsals start at Onassis ONX on the 16th, and then it's showtime. Here's what I've been up to while I've been out here (followed up with some recommended shows from good friends and past collaborators).

Matt

September: locking in on writing

One floor of a five-story former factory, a laptop, a legal pad. Nothing to do but write.

Bemis gives us a live/work studio but there was also an entire floor of a factory available to us so of course I decided to use that. September was all script: taking the earlier work-in-progress showings, figuring out what worked, and expanding the story into something bigger, bolder, scarier, and funnier than it was before. A lot of it happened on paper first, and then I transferred it to a google doc (off-line mode so I could stay focused).

This was absolutely a staged photo. Photo: Colin Conces.

October: getting my coding game off the ground

Then the script had to become a game. A working text adventure that also interfaces with the lights, sound, and projection systems of the theater. To do this I needed 3 monitors (4 if you include my laptop). I know it looks excessive but I assure you it was completely necessary.

“Pretend you're coding or something.” Photo: Colin Conces.

If you're curious how the show is built or what's running under the hood, reply to this email. I love talking about this stuff, and maybe a post-show email will do a breakdown of the system for all you tech heads.

Once there was a solid rough draft, our director Jerry Lieblich flew out for a three-day work sprint. We got a huge amount done, got very excited about where the show is heading, and also did some extremely serious research.

Research. Jerry and me surviving the Dungeon of the Damned.

On Jerry's last night we ran a rough draft of the show for the Bemis staff and residents in Low End, their music venue in the basement. We learned a lot. Most importantly, it seems like this show has some real juice (milk?). Getting to hear the sound design thus far on the music venue's sound system was extremely exciting. The jump scares really jump. ;)

Run-through attendees praised the depth and layered critical dialogue of the piece.

I'm spending my last week here editing the script, building in new ideas, and working with our wonderful designers and production team to flesh out every sound, light, video, and set moment before rehearsals start.

“Just keep typing.” Photo: Colin Conces.

Meanwhile, Omaha

It may sound like I mostly worked, but I also got to have fun in Omaha. We went to galas. We hosted quite a few barbecues. I made some new friends. A few highlights:

The Bemis gala. Photo: Ben Semisch.

Pumpkin Destruction Day.

My partner Benita (an accomplished pumpkin destroyer) visited from New York City over Halloween weekend. We went to Vala's Pumpkin Patch, where every November 1, the day after Halloween, it opens its fields to the public to enact carnage on every pumpkin that didn't get picked. It was a blast. (Although the rotting stench of massacred pumpkins starts to get intense.)

Carnage.

The Alpine Inn.

A bar on the edge of town that feeds its leftover fried chicken to raccoons. Very large and plump raccoons right outside the window, while you eat your own fried chicken. It's a lot. It is also, I think, a must-visit. (Shout out to Peter Burr for the recommendation)

Our plump and furry friends / Katja Zimmer posing outside the storied establishment.

Barbecues galore. You can see all the other Bemis residents and their work on the Bemis residency page.

Sad my time here is coming to an end, but grateful to Bemis and all the people I met here for such an incredible, warm, and productive residency.
